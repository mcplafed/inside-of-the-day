Your AI bill has a hidden discount.

Up to 90% off cached input. Are you comparing cache prices too?

When your apps reuse the same instructions, tool definitions or documents, cached input can be a big part of the bill. And newer models can be much cheaper here too.

This isn’t just a price trade-off. Newer models can outperform their predecessors while costing less. Check that advantage on your own tasks, not just a benchmark.

A few swaps worth testing today. USD per 1M tokens. Input / output; cache read:

• Claude Haiku 4.5 → 5.5: input/output $1/$5 → $0.10/$0.50; cache $0.10 → $0.01 (−90%*)
• GPT-5.4 mini → GPT-6 Luna: input/output $0.75/$4.50 → $0.10/$0.50; cache $0.075 → $0.01 (−87%)
• GPT-5.4 → GPT-6.1 Terra: input/output $2.50/$15 → $1/$5; cache $0.25 → $0.05 (−80%)
• GPT-5.5 → GPT-6.1 Sol: input/output $5/$30 → $2/$10; cache $0.50 → $0.10 (−80%)
• Claude Sonnet 4.5 → 5.5: input/output $3/$15 → $2/$10; cache $0.30 → $0.10 (−67%)

The cache needs to warm up after a switch, and writes have their own price. The actual saving depends on how often your prompts are reused, cache hits, output tokens and retries.

So don’t just compare the price list. Test on your own traffic and measure the cost per successful task.
